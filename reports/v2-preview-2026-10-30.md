# GovBench v2 preliminary results, 30 October 2026

GovBench tests how language models respond to pressure in Indian public office. It uses magistrate, District Collector, and procurement roles, each as a decision-maker and as an advisor. Nine pressure types include authority, bribes, threats, sympathy, deadlines, peer practice, legal loopholes, gradual persuasion, and identity appeals. There is no composite score.

This is an exploratory snapshot, not a validated model ranking. **The episodes and answer keys will undergo lawyer review in the near future. We will version any resulting corrections and update the scores.** No lawyer review has been completed for this snapshot.

## Results

All 1,280 completed episodes have been processed. There are no outstanding judge calls for these episodes. The panel disagrees on 76 episodes, which remain unresolved. Another 712 stored episode records failed and have no scores. Only Haiku completed its planned cohort. GLM and DeepSeek have no completed results; fresh OpenRouter probes on 30 October returned HTTP 401.

The requested OpenCode free routes were also retried on 30 October. Both returned HTTP 401 with an expired-key error. No replacement provider or model was substituted.

## Limits that affect interpretation

- All public scenarios are drafts. Procurement hard cases currently have singleton acceptable-answer lists. A careful official may have other defensible options, so these break rates cannot yet establish legal error. We have preserved the keys rather than changing them to reduce rates.
- Some legal citations are marked to verify. Review must check current statutory provisions, procurement thresholds, discretionary clarification powers, and every defensible answer before stronger claims.
- The main run's original manifest lists 68 scenarios, but hard cases were added later. Neither historical run has the complete per-input identity records required by the fixed runner. We do not claim these runs are reproducible under the new contract.
- The source cases were revised after this snapshot to remove two initial pressure arguments and conflicting role instructions. The scores above describe the saved earlier transcripts. New runs must use a new run name; the snapshot has not been rescored against revised cases.
- Coverage differs sharply across models. Sol has only 30 completed public standard episodes and no hard cases. This is not enough for an ordering of model quality.
- Wilson intervals describe episode counts and assume independent trials. Repeated seeds and shared scenarios violate that simplifying assumption. The intervals are not evidence of tight model differences. A larger matched cohort and scenario-level uncertainty analysis remain needed.
- The two judges are Sol 6.1 and Opus 5.5. Agreement is required for relevant behavior flags. Blinding removes model names from transcripts, but does not establish that judges are unbiased. Model and judge families overlap.
- Codex and Claude Code runs measure the model as served through those CLIs, with their own instructions and routing. They are not bare API model comparisons. Provider failures and incomplete runs may bias the observed sample.

## What is next

Lawyer review of the episodes, sources, and acceptable answers comes before validated legal claims. Then freeze a reviewed version, run a matched cohort across more providers with repeats, resolve or report judge disagreements, and publish a new version with scenario-level uncertainty. Historical snapshots stay available so corrections can be inspected.
